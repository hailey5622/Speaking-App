## 2. Why We Are Building It (왜 만드는가)

**My answer:**

- Problem: Students feel nervous when meeting foreign friends and struggle to start conversations in English.
- Solution: Students practice greetings and small talk through **Listen, Speak, Feedback, Try again.**
- Goal: Students can say five common expressions for meeting new foreign friends intelligibly.

---

## 3. Target Users (대상 사용자)


**My answer:**

- Grade: University students
- Level: CEFR B1–B2
- Characteristic: Wants to find interesting conversation topics with new people, avoid grammatical mistakes, and speak naturally.
- Common mistake: Translating directly from Korean and using unnatural expressions in conversations.
- Use context: On a smartphone, 5–7 minutes, for speaking practice.

## 5. Key Features (주요 기능)


**My answer:**
1. See the situation — A foreign friend's message appears in a chat bubble with Korean meaning and audio.
2. Listen — [🔊 Listen] plays the friend's message, and [🐢 Slow] plays it slowly.
3. Speak — Tap [🎤 Mic] to reply; the spoken response appears as a chat bubble.
4. Compare — Compare the original reply with the corrected version, focusing on grammar and natural expressions.
5. Feedback — The app provides brief feedback and explains how to make the reply sound more natural.
6. Try again / Next — Students can choose [Try Again] [Other Response] [Next Topic] to continue the conversation.

After all sentences are finished, the app moves to the Result Screen.

## 7. Feedback Rules (피드백 규칙)


**✏️ My answer:**

- **Character personality / speaking style:** Friendly, supportive, and casual, like a university student chatting with a new foreign friend.
- **My rules:**
1. Prioritize whether the partner understands the message and whether the response fits the conversation.
2. Correct minor grammar mistakes, such as missing articles (a, the), on the screen without counting them as errors.
3. Do not count minor mistakes that do not affect meaning or communication.
4. Give brief, encouraging feedback in simple English with Korean translations. Praise first and focus on only one improvement at a time.
5. Change feedback by attempt: 1st recast (provide a natural version) → 2nd elicitation (give a first-letter hint) → 3rd chunking (break the sentence into short, manageable phrases).

| Situation                 | Feedback (English + Korean)                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Success                   | Nice! Your reply fits the conversation naturally. (좋아요! 대화 맥락에 잘 맞는 자연스러운 답변이에요!)                                                                |
| Success + small error     | Good job! I understood you clearly. Here's a slightly more natural version: "[corrected sentence]." (잘했어요! 의미가 명확하게 전달됐어요. 이렇게 말하면 조금 더 자연스러워요.) |
| 1st attempt (Recast)      | Good try! You could say, "[natural sentence]." (잘 시도했어요! 이렇게 말하면 더 자연스러워요.)                                                                      |
| 2nd attempt (Elicitation) | You're getting closer! Try starting with "[first-letter hint]..." (점점 좋아지고 있어요! 이 힌트로 시작해 보세요.)                                                  |
| 3rd attempt (Chunking)    | Almost there! Let's try it in small parts: "[chunk 1] / [chunk 2]." (거의 다 왔어요! 짧은 부분으로 나눠서 말해 봐요.)                                               |
| No speech recognized      | Take your time! Try speaking again when you're ready. (천천히 해도 괜찮아요! 준비되면 다시 말해 보세요.)                                                             |

## 8. Do Not (하지 말 것)

**My answer:**

### Privacy and Safety (required — keep these)
- Do not save or send audio recordings.
- Do not collect personal information (name, student ID, school, email). No login or sign-up.
- No ads, external links, or payment features.
- No photos of real people or famous characters.

### Learning Design (add at least 2)
- Do not count minor grammar mistakes, such as missing articles (a or the), as errors unless they affect understanding.
- Do not give long explanations or correct multiple things at once. Give brief, encouraging feedback in simple English with Korean translations, focusing on one improvement at a time.
- Do not require users to repeat the exact example sentence. Accept different answers if they are understandable and appropriate for the conversation.
- Do not use overly formal, textbook-like expressions. Prioritize natural, casual English used when meeting new university friends.

### Other (optional)
- Do not use numerical scores or percentages. Evaluate responses based on understandability and conversational relevance.
